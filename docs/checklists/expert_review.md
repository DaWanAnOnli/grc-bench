# Expert review by Qoherent: what to check and how to record it

Implements eval plan §5.3 ("Expert review. A Qoherent expert reviews every GNU Radio question and
every Tier-F and Tier-A task for correctness and relevance before the freeze: about 30 hours of
expert time"), with §8.2 (Table 7), §12 (cut list) and RQ1b (H1b: experts accept at least 90% of
the items as correct and relevant).

This page is for the Qoherent engineer who reviews GRC-Bench items. It says what to look at, how
long it should take, and how to record each verdict. The team prepares the items and acts on your
verdicts.

## Why your review matters

- **Correctness:** you know GNU Radio better than any of us. You catch questions with a wrong answer
  and tasks with a wrong reference or a broken check.
- **Relevance:** you tell us whether an item tests something a GNU Radio developer actually needs.
- **Evidence for the paper:** your verdicts are evidence. The share of items you accept as correct
  and relevant tests hypothesis H1b (at least 90%). It is reported as it is, whatever the result.

## Ground rules

- **Do not paste any item into ChatGPT, Claude or any other model or online tool.** Test items are
  never sent to a model outside a declared run (eval plan §6). Please also keep the items inside
  Qoherent until the benchmark is released.
- **Judge the item as a GNU Radio 3.10 user would meet it.** The harness pins GNU Radio 3.10.12 and
  GRC-Agent at commit `6c98128`.
- **Who drafted the items.** Please judge every item exactly as you would judge a human-written one.
  - **Tier-F tasks:** Claude (Anthropic) drafted:
    - the instructions;
    - the reference ("golden") flowgraphs: written from scratch for the composed tasks, and adapted
      from a GNU Radio example for the others;
    - the oracles, and their plain-words summary (`spec_properties`).
  - **Tier-A tasks:** Claude drafts the task specs, the scripted replies, the end-state checks and
    the golden tool-call sequences.
  - Two team members review and edit every task before you see it. Each record says who drafted it
    in `provenance.drafted_by`.
  - **This differs from what the Data Summary (§11) told Qoherent.** Example A says that correct
    programs come from GNU Radio's examples, open community projects or team members, and that
    DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash drafts each build instruction. Example D says that team members write each
    agent task and its checks, with no AI model involved. The team is raising these deviations with
    Qoherent. If you have concerns about them, please tell us.
  - The GNU Radio block questions are generated by a script, and their answers come from running
    GNU Radio: the script runs the block as the question states and reads its output.
  - The 185 frozen test questions and the 200 dev questions come from Qoherent's dataset.
- **You review after the team.** Two team members have already reviewed each task (see
  [tier_f_task_review.md](tier_f_task_review.md) and
  [tier_a_task_review.md](tier_a_task_review.md)). You see the version they approved.

time. Please note how long these decisions take; the team records the hours and reports them.

## What to check

For every item, answer two questions: **is it correct?** and **is it relevant?** Then give a
verdict. The lists below say what "correct" and "relevant" mean for each kind of item.

### GNU Radio block questions (script-generated)

Each question asks what a real GNU Radio 3.10 block does. The block is set as the question states
and receives a stated finite input. The question asks for one number:

- the value of its k-th output item, for example the 3rd output of Keep M in N on the input 1, 2, …, 10;
- or how many items it produces, for example how many vectors Stream to Vector makes from 80 items.

The answer key is GNU Radio's own output: a script ran the block twice and read the recording. An
independent model of the block's documented behaviour gave the same number.

Correct:

- [ ] The question states everything the answer depends on: the block, every parameter that matters,
      the input values and their order, and which output item or port is meant.
- [ ] The answer is what GNU Radio 3.10 produces for that setting and input.
- [ ] The answer does not hinge on an implementation detail a developer could not know. One example
      is a filter's start-up transient, so the script drops resampler counts, which depend on it.

Relevant:

- [ ] A GNU Radio developer needs to know this behaviour in real work, for example to predict what
      a block does to a stream, its rate change or its tap conventions.
- [ ] It is not trivia.

### GNU Radio questions among the frozen test questions and the dev questions

These come from Qoherent's dataset. The team writes a rubric for each one: the points a correct
answer must contain, the errors that make it wrong, and any numeric tolerance (eval plan §5.2). The
dev questions are checked in the same way; the sheet marks them with `split` = `dev`.

Correct:

- [ ] The question is well-posed for GNU Radio 3.10. It has no false premise and no block, parameter
      or tool that does not exist.
- [ ] It can be answered without assumptions the question does not state.
- [ ] The rubric's points are right and complete. Its disqualifying errors are real errors.

Relevant:

- [ ] A GNU Radio developer could plausibly ask or need this.

### Tier-F tasks (flowgraphs: build, modify, repair, parameter-tune)

Each task has:

- an instruction;
- for modify, repair and parameter-tune tasks, a starting flowgraph;
- a reference ("golden") flowgraph;
- a behavioural check (the oracle). The oracle runs the model's flowgraph headless and measures its
  output, for example the frequency of the main peak.

The model answers by making **one edit** to the open flowgraph through GRC-Agent's `change_graph`
tool, after at most one documentation lookup.

Correct:

- [ ] The reference flowgraph is a sensible solution, one you would accept from a colleague.
- [ ] The oracle checks what the instruction asks for. Its tolerances are what an engineer would
      accept, neither so loose that a wrong design passes nor so tight that a good one fails.
- [ ] The specification is physically sensible:
  - sample rates and Nyquist limits are respected;
  - filter specifications are achievable;
  - the amplitudes are realistic.
- [ ] Other reasonable solutions an experienced user would choose would pass. Please name any you
      think would fail; the team then tests them.
- [ ] For repair tasks, the fault looks like a real mistake, and the instruction's description of
      the symptom is accurate.

Relevant:

- [ ] This is a task a GNU Radio user would really do.
- [ ] Its level matches its difficulty: L1 a short single-rate chain; L2 rate changes, modulation
      and filtering; L3 large or multi-rate graphs.

### Tier-A tasks (GRC-Agent using its tools)

Each task has:

- a user request;
- a starting state (an open flowgraph and some files);
- scripted replies for the questions the agent may ask;
- end-state checks on the final saved flowgraph.

A correct run takes 8–20 tool calls.

Correct:

- [ ] The request is clear, and everything needed is in it or in the starting state.
- [ ] The end-state checks capture "done" the way the user would judge it. Any valid way of reaching
      the goal passes.
- [ ] The scripted replies are what a real user would answer.

Relevant:

- [ ] The task belongs to one of Qoherent's categories (build, repair, parameter tuning,
      Embedded-Python blocks, modify, knowledge, save-block, file operations, run-and-verify), or is
      one of the few refusal tasks.
- [ ] It resembles real GRC-Agent use.
- [ ] It does not share a goal or a starting flowgraph with one of Qoherent's own collection tasks
      (t01–t15, t16, t22, t24, t25) or GRC-Agent's test scenarios. You know these better than we
      do.

## How you receive the items

For each batch the team sends:

- a **packet** per item with everything you need to judge it. A task packet includes:
  - the instruction;
  - the starting and reference `.grc` files, which open in GNU Radio Companion;
  - the checks in plain words (`spec_properties`), with their tolerances;
  - for Tier A, the scripted replies and the end-state checks.
- the **sheet** to fill in: a copy of
  [`expert_review.csv`](../../handoff/review_sheets/templates/expert_review.csv) with one row per
  item. The team fills in `item_id`, `item_kind`, `split` and `packet_ref`.

## How to record your verdict

Fill in one row per item:

| Column | Values | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `correct` | `yes`, `no`, `unsure` | is the item right as it stands (question and answer, or task, reference and checks)? |
| `relevant` | `yes`, `no`, `unsure` | does it test something a GNU Radio developer needs? |
| `verdict` | `accept`, `accept with edits`, `reject` | your overall decision; empty while `correct` or `relevant` is `unsure` |
| `reason_code` | `wrong_answer`, `ambiguous_wording`, `broken_oracle`, `not_relevant`, `outdated`, `other` | required when `correct` or `relevant` is `no`, or the verdict is `accept with edits` or `reject`; several codes are separated by `;` |
| `comment` | text | what is wrong, in a sentence or two |
| `suggested_fix` | text | the change that would make the item acceptable, if you see one |
| `minutes` | number | the time you spent on the item (it helps us plan batch 2) |
| `expert`, `date` | initials, YYYY-MM-DD | |
| `team_action`, `team_action_by` | filled in by the team | what was done with your verdict |

How to choose:

- **`accept`:** correct and relevant, with no change needed.
- **`accept with edits`:** correct and relevant once the edit in `suggested_fix` is made. Use this
  for wording fixes and small changes, such as a tolerance or a missing detail.
- **`reject`:** wrong in a way an edit cannot fix, or not relevant.

**If you are unsure.** `unsure` is not a verdict. When you cannot decide without information you
do not have:

- write `unsure` in `correct` or `relevant` (or both);
- leave `verdict` empty;
- say in `comment` what information you need.

The team follows it up, and you give the verdict later.

The reason codes follow eval plan §5.3. An item is removed only for a reason a person can state:
wrong answer, ambiguous wording or broken oracle. We add three more:

- `not_relevant`;
- `outdated`, for behaviour that differs in GNU Radio 3.10;
- `other`.

## What happens with your verdicts

- **`reject`:** the team removes the item, or rewrites it as a new version (the original is kept),
  and records the reason. Removed items are counted and published with their reasons (eval plan
  §5.3).
- **`accept with edits`:** the team applies the edit, re-runs the item's automatic checks, and sends
  it back to you if the edit changes the answer or the grading.
- **`unsure` in `correct` or `relevant`, `verdict` empty:** the team gets the information you
  asked for and sends the item back to you. Your later verdict is recorded in a new row (the
  first row is kept).
- **H1b:** your first-pass verdicts are used to test hypothesis H1b. The pre-registration fixes the
  exact counting rule. Our proposal:
  - an item counts as accepted when `correct` and `relevant` are both `yes` and the verdict is
    `accept` or `accept with edits`;
  - an item marked `unsure` on the first pass counts as **not accepted**. We report how many
    there were, and the share accepted after the follow-up beside the first-pass share;
  - we also report the share accepted with no edit.
- **Team review takes priority over statistics.** Item statistics from the base and panel models
  (difficulty, discrimination; eval plan §5.3) may flag an item for another look. They never remove
  an item on their own.

## 2. Sources

- **20 composed tasks**, written from scratch on textbook themes. Their split is the split their signature-v2 cell hashes to.
- **10 tasks from GNU Radio 3.10.12 example flowgraphs** (GPL-3.0-or-later), converted to run headless and with their numbers changed (`perturbation` in each task). Each example is assigned to one split at file level (`splits/flowgraph_seed_split.json`), and a task uses only examples of its own split.
- **192 generated tasks** (`./gb tier-f generate`). A seeded generator draws a chain of stages on a set of tones, computes every expected feature exactly from the tones, and keeps a task only if its cell hashes to the task's split and the task passes every gate. Each task's `build.py` rebuilds it from its recorded attempt. An L2 task has a cell of its own. A single-rate chain of at most 5 blocks has few function combinations, and the retune template of L3 reaches few cells, so tasks of one split may share a cell: at most 11 L1 tasks and 2 L3 tasks per cell. Tasks of one cell share a `cluster_id`, and the clustered standard errors count the cell once: the test split has 127 cells, the dev split 40.

## 3. Split discipline

Signature v2 describes a flowgraph by the set of functions its blocks perform, so reordering blocks or adding an inert block (a Throttle) does not change it (`reports/tier_f_signature_v2.md`). A composed task's split must equal the split its reference cell hashes to. An example task's split comes from its example file, so its cell may hash to any split; that cell is reserved like every other test and dev cell. A generated task follows the composed rule.

- Tasks whose split disagrees with the rule: **0**.
- Groups of tasks that share a reference cell across splits: **0**.
- Reserved cells (reference and variant cells of test and dev tasks, plus the start and golden end-state cells of the Tier-A tasks, kept out of training data): **206** (140 hash to test, 43 to dev, 23 to train). The training team excludes all of them.

## 5. Reviews

- **Generated tasks (192):** checked by the automatic gates, then reviewed like every task.
- **The review protocol:** two review agents per task, each running its own alternative solutions through `./gb ladder`; the expert agent decided the disagreements, merged the edits and audited a sample of the agreements. The decided edits are applied through the generator and per-task patches (`reviews/tier_f_patches.json`), and every task passed the gates again after them.
- **First review:** one review per hand-drafted task (30 records, `reports/tier_f_reviews/round1/<task>.json`). Each finding was accepted or rejected with a reason in `<task>.resolution.json`; the set-level plan is `reports/tier_f_reviews/fix_plan.md`.
- **Second review:** one reviewer per group of three tasks (10 groups, `reports/tier_f_reviews/round2/<group>.json`). Its findings are resolved in the `round2` entry of the task's resolution record. Group g2 (gbf-test-03, gbf-dev-02, gbf-dev-05) had a checklist pass instead.
- Each task's `review.notes` gives the review's decision, then the oracle's known limits.
